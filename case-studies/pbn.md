# Promotional Broadcast Network: Building Experiences That Continued After Release

## The Project

At Promotional Broadcast Network, I served as Art Director and Lead Programmer on a narrative-driven adventure game for both Mac and Windows.

The experience was inspired by exploration games such as *Myst* and *Riven*.

It combined 3D environments, animation, storytelling, interaction, and software development into a single entertainment experience.

I directed a team of four artists and programmers while also contributing directly to modeling, texturing, animation, rendering, programming, camera direction, color, and the overall visual language of the game.

Our 3D workflow evolved from Strata 3D to LightWave as the project grew.

---

## Keeping the World Consistent

A project built by several artists can easily begin to feel like several different worlds. Part of my responsibility as Art Director was **maintaining consistency**: camera choices, lighting, color palettes, environment design, and interaction.

Those decisions had to reinforce one another so that **the player experienced a single coherent place** rather than a collection of independently produced assets.

---

## An Experience That Could Change

One of the more unusual technical features was an online update system. Although the primary game shipped on CD-ROM, it could connect online and receive new information. That allowed content to change or become available over time rather than remaining permanently frozen at the moment the disc shipped.

Today, continuously updated software is ordinary. But at the time, the distinction between packaged software and online experiences was much stronger.

Building a system that crossed that boundary opened up interesting possibilities for storytelling. The experience didn't necessarily have to end when the software shipped. It also gave us, the artists and developers, a little leeway. While we needed to have assests locked down when the CD was pressed, we still had a couple weeks after that to continue refining and testing before the game went live. 

That was a luxury compared to many of today's continuous-integration pipelines. 

---

## Looking Back

The tools we used are obviously very different from the ones I use today, but the underlying problems aren't.

- How do several disciplines contribute to **one coherent experience**?
- How do visual design, interaction, technology, and story **reinforce** each other?
- How can technology **support an idea without becoming the idea** itself?

Those are questions I've continued asking throughout my career.
